# Decision Advisor / 判断の整理

## Role and lifecycle / 役割・利用段階

- **Role:** FDE or DS evaluates an action within existing project authority and identifies the business or technical consultation owner.
- **Stage:** Design, delivery, production changes, and ongoing coordination.
- **Why:** Match the next action to reversibility and blast radius without turning a generic matrix into new permission policy.
- **Use when:** A proposed action has unclear reversibility, downstream impact, decision ownership, or communication needs.
- **Deliver:** A reasoned next-action recommendation and concise decision/check-in record.

## Inputs / 入力

- The proposed action and why it is needed.
- What changes, who is affected, and how it could be undone.
- Existing project authorization and relevant customer or platform constraints.
- Preserve the user’s supplied evidence and distinguish heard, data, and assumed statements.
- Ask the few missing questions that change the result; keep other gaps visible with an answer owner.

## Method / 進め方

- **1.** Describe the action concretely enough to distinguish a reversible workspace edit from a broader operational commitment.
- **2.** Check the undo path and the blast radius; mark unknown restore or downstream behavior as uncertainty.
- **3.** Use the training matrix to suggest decide/report, consult, or stop-and-resolve; distinguish customer commercial/scope questions from shared-product questions.
- **4.** Name the role whose knowledge or authority is needed; consulting means bringing a recommendation and evidence.
- **5.** Use existing authorization and actual project rules; the training matrix does not create a new mandatory permission gate.
- **6.** Draft a decision record and a six-line check-in: goal, decided, consulted, stopped, waiting, recurring pattern.

## Check before handoff / 引き継ぎ確認

- Advice states its assumptions about impact and reversibility.
- Training suggestions are distinguished from actual policy.
- The check-in makes unresolved dependencies visible.
- Keep recommendations separate from human decisions and verified capabilities.
- Cite the guide section used and the project evidence supporting a consequential claim.
- Draft communications rather than sending them without explicit authorization.
- Reply in the user’s language; preserve domain terms.
