# Ownership & Handoff Designer / 責任分担・引き継ぎ

## Role and lifecycle / 役割・利用段階

- **Role:** DS owns the business outcome; FDE owns technical delivery; adjacent roles need explicit decision/delivery boundaries.
- **Stage:** Scoping, sales-to-delivery handoff, build planning, launch, maintenance, and account transitions.
- **Why:** Prevent important work or decisions from falling between teams when several roles contribute.
- **Use when:** Clarify role boundaries, test an ownership agreement, or prepare a business/technical handoff.
- **Deliver:** A responsibility map, scenario-tested boundary statement, and receiving-owner handoff checklist.

## Inputs / 入力

- Customer outcome, lifecycle stage, and current roles.
- A boundary dispute or handoff that needs to work.
- Known agreements and unresolved ownership questions.
- Preserve the user’s supplied evidence and distinguish heard, data, and assumed statements.
- Ask the few missing questions that change the result; keep other gaps visible with an answer owner.

## Method / 進め方

- **1.** Name who owns the customer outcome even when other people deliver parts of the work.
- **2.** Separate who decides from who performs each activity; include business scoping, technical design/build, commercial commitments, adoption, and maintenance.
- **3.** Draft a boundary in plain language: what one role owns and what the adjacent role owns.
- **4.** Test the boundary against a concrete scenario such as an unscoped sales promise, declining usage, or an expansion request.
- **5.** Record contradictory responsibilities and the roles whose agreement would resolve them; do not invent a final organization policy.
- **6.** Create a handoff checklist that preserves the problem, intended outcome, evidence, open questions, and receiving owner.

## Check before handoff / 引き継ぎ確認

- Each important decision has a named role or an explicit gap.
- The receiving team can explain what it is taking over.
- Draft boundaries are labeled proposed until agreed.
- Keep recommendations separate from human decisions and verified capabilities.
- Cite the guide section used and the project evidence supporting a consequential claim.
- Draft communications rather than sending them without explicit authorization.
- Reply in the user’s language; preserve domain terms.
