# Customer Conversation Rehearsal / 顧客対話の練習

## Role and lifecycle / 役割・利用段階

- **Role:** FDE explains technical value and proof; DS owns the commercial next step and customer outcome.
- **Stage:** Discovery, solution review, adoption conversations, alternatives evaluation, expansion, and renewal.
- **Why:** Understand the customer’s objection before answering and connect the response to their actual work.
- **Use when:** Rehearse an objection, discuss an existing alternative, explain production value, or prepare a proof demonstration.
- **Deliver:** A question-led response, demonstration plan, and behavior-based rehearsal feedback.

## Inputs / 入力

- What was said, by which role, and what you said just before it.
- What the customer already uses and what they want to change.
- Available proof and the next useful conversation or demonstration.
- Preserve the user’s supplied evidence and distinguish heard, data, and assumed statements.
- Ask the few missing questions that change the result; keep other gaps visible with an answer owner.

## Method / 進め方

- **1.** Clarify what the objection means: existing spend, poor past results, ecosystem preference, sufficient current tools, or confidence in building internally are hypotheses to test.
- **2.** Acknowledge what is true about the existing choice; avoid an unsupported feature contest.
- **3.** Ask what work they have done with it and where the workflow succeeds or stalls.
- **4.** Reflect their answer in their vocabulary; connect the gap to a decision or outcome they care about.
- **5.** Offer a specific demonstration or evidence check; hand commercial scope, pricing, and promises to the appropriate owner.
- **6.** Rehearse with the assistant as the customer; give feedback on whether the response acknowledged, asked, reframed, and showed a next step.

## Check before handoff / 引き継ぎ確認

- The response is grounded in this customer’s work.
- The proposed proof can actually be shown.
- Rehearsal feedback identifies observable behavior.
- Keep recommendations separate from human decisions and verified capabilities.
- Cite the guide section used and the project evidence supporting a consequential claim.
- Draft communications rather than sending them without explicit authorization.
- Reply in the user’s language; preserve domain terms.
