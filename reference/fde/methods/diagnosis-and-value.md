# Diagnosis, value, and decision communication / 原因・価値・意思決定

## Two directions / 二つの方向

- Investigating downward asks why the observed problem occurred.
- Investigating upward asks what it costs and whose result is affected.
- Causes may branch; five questions are a prompt rather than a fixed stopping count.
- Check each causal claim using an observation, test, or explicitly marked assumption.
- A requested report or feature is a starting point for understanding the work.

## The value chain / 価値の連鎖

- Start with the operator’s reported difficulty.
- Identify the decision, delay, rework, or error it causes.
- Identify the operational outcome that would change if the problem were solved.
- Connect that outcome to a measure a business owner is accountable for.
- Keep each intermediate link visible instead of jumping from technical quality to revenue.

## Three measurement levels / 三段階の指標

- **Activity:** whether people use the system and which work it processes.
- **Outcome:** the operational change, such as reduced rework or decision delay.
- **Impact:** the business result, such as avoidable cost or delivery performance.
- For every measure, define population, period, unit, calculation, source, baseline, and owner.
- If the baseline is missing, propose a collection method.
- Treat a value calculation as derived from its supplied inputs, not as measured impact.

## Steering / 運営会議

- A steering discussion puts results and decisions in front of people who can act.
- Keep the discussion about customer value, consequential risks, and requested decisions.
- Useful inputs include the baseline, observed result, evidence limits, and next recommendation.
- Routine build progress belongs only where it explains a decision or risk.
- An operator’s satisfaction does not establish that the budget owner has seen the value.

## Escalation / 支援の依頼

- Escalate a blocker when help or a decision is needed beyond the current owner’s control.
- State the situation, what was tried, the exact help needed, the owner role, the required date, and the consequence of delay.
- Escalate an opportunity as a proposal with a value hypothesis and scope implication.
- Finding an opportunity does not authorize adding it to committed scope.
- Drafting an escalation does not send it.

## Example / 例

- A team reports high usage of an extraction tool.
- Ask which work changed and whether exceptions or manual re-entry decreased.
- If no outcome is measured, propose a baseline and customer owner.
- Present that measurement decision to the steering group rather than describing usage as proven savings.
