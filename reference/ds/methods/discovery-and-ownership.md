# Discovery and responsibility / 業務発見と責任

## The role / 役割

- A deployment strategist owns the customer outcome through adoption and measurement.
- An FDE leads the technical delivery behind that outcome.
- A DS can delegate work while retaining responsibility for ensuring the result has an owner.
- Distinguish outcome ownership from sales, presales, project scheduling, and standard-product enablement.
- The exact boundary depends on the organization and account.

## A concrete-day interview / 実際の一日の聞き取り

- Ask for one recent working day, starting at its beginning.
- Ask what the person decided and what information they used.
- Ask what took longest and what they repeated.
- Ask what happened next and whose help they needed.
- Follow handoffs between people and systems.
- Use the customer’s words before translating them into proposed system capabilities.
- Finish the account of what happened before introducing solutions.

## Observation and interpretation / 観察と解釈

- A reported event is heard evidence until verified.
- A supplied log or measured result can support a data claim within its scope.
- A proposed explanation remains assumed until checked.
- Playback lets the customer correct the interpretation before it becomes a requirement.

## Ownership boundaries / 分担

- Separate who decides from who does the work.
- Test a proposed boundary against a concrete case.
- Useful cases include an unscoped promise, declining adoption, an expansion request, and a missing maintenance owner.
- Record contradictions instead of silently assigning them to whichever role is present.
- Identify which adjacent roles need to agree a proposed boundary.

## Handoff / 引き継ぎ

- Preserve the underlying problem, intended outcome, scope, evidence, and constraints.
- Name the receiving owner and unresolved questions.
- Identify business and technical responsibilities after launch.
- A completed handoff is not evidence that the customer achieved the outcome.

## Example / 例

- A user repeats a reconciliation step after an automated result arrives.
- The interview records the event and the information they distrust.
- The next investigation asks whether source quality, decision rules, or interface behavior causes the repetition.
- The skill does not assume automation is the answer before that investigation.
