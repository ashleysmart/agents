## Slide 12

- THE ROLE What a DS is, and is not
- Not a consultant
- Not a salesperson
- Not a project manager
- A DS owns the outcome
- The relationship serves the outcome, not the other way round.
- Delivering on time to a spec is necessary, not sufficient.
- A consultant leaves a recommendation. A DS leaves a system people use.
- A capable product is worth nothing until it is used. The DS owns it from the first conversation until the result can be measured, then grows the account.

## Slide 21

- THE DS ' S FIRST SKILL How a DS interviews: one real day
- 1 "Walk me through one day last week, from the start."
- Three rules
- A real day: typical days are tidier than real ones (contextual inquiry, 1998).
- 2 "What did you decide, and what did you look at?"
- Decisions are where the value is (Critical Decision Method, 1989).
- Ask what happened, not what they think or would do (The Mom Test, [identity redacted], 2013)
- 3 "What took longest? What did you do twice?"
- Slow, repeated work is where AI and automation help most.
- Do not suggest solutions during the interview
- 4 "Then what happened? Who did you need?"
- Write down their words, not yours
- Follow the work from person to person. It shows who else is involved.

## Slide 35

- BOUNDARY LINES · EXAMPLE Testing a line: an example
- 1 Start with the line on the last slide
- "A product manager builds for many customers; a DS builds for one customer."
- 2 Test it on a case
- Three customers ask for the same custom report. Whose job is it to decide what happens?
- 3 It fails
- The DS hears it from one customer and the PM hears it from all three. Both can claim it, so nobody decides.
- 4 Rewrite it
- "…; a PM decides when custom work becomes product (the Three Client Rule), using the evidence the DS brings."
- 5 Test it again
- The PM decides. The DS brings the three requests. Two people reading the line now give the same answer.
- Expect your first line to fail a case. That is why we test it.
